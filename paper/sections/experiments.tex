\section{Experimentation and Initial Results}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Setup}

Each T$k$ is compared with its own T0 (not with T$k\!-\!1$), so scores express cumulative
divergence from the human original, averaged over the 700 documents.
\textbf{Wording kept:} BLEU-4~\cite{papineni2002bleu} (smoothed) and ROUGE-L F1~\cite{lin2004rouge}.
\textbf{Meaning kept:} BERTScore F1~\cite{zhang2020bertscore} (distilbert-base-uncased) and SBERT
cosine~\cite{reimers2019sentencebert} (all-MiniLM-L6-v2). Raw BERTScore gives even unrelated text
$\approx$0.67, so we rescale it with the official baseline, $(F-0.666)/(1-0.666)$, mapping
unrelated text to $\approx$0. A faithful paraphrase scores low on BLEU and ROUGE-L, so we read
them only as measures of surface replacement.

For ChatGPT (Table~\ref{tab:semantic_metrics}), BLEU-4 loses 86\% of its value while SBERT loses
only 12\%, and 94\% of the BLEU drop already happens at T1; later rounds change little.
Figure~\ref{fig:trajectories} shows that \textbf{every paraphraser discards wording faster than
meaning}, but at different rates. ChatGPT keeps the same meaning as PaLM2 at T3 (SBERT 0.875 vs.\
0.876) with half its word overlap (BLEU 0.14 vs.\ 0.28): it rewrites most aggressively at no
semantic cost. Default DIPPER loses the most of both (BLEU 0.06, SBERT 0.72). The rule-based
WordNet baseline moves the opposite way to the LLMs, keeping the most wording (BLEU 0.40) but
losing more meaning (SBERT 0.79): synonym swaps keep the skeleton and break the sense, so
simulated paraphrasing is no substitute for real LLM chains. DIPPER (high) scores SBERT 0.06, the
level of unrelated text, confirming the preprocessing check.

\textbf{Domain matters} (Table~\ref{tab:domain_metrics}). Science, summaries and news keep the most
meaning: terms, entities and numbers must be copied, which anchors the content. Stories and
opinions keep the least, since they leave room to restructure narrative and rhetoric. The effect
depends on the paraphraser: PaLM2 copies heavily in science (BLEU 0.50) but rewrites opinion
almost fully (0.11), while DIPPER-low is nearly flat across domains.

\subsection{RQ1: Which Planks Are Replaced First?}
\label{sec:rq1}

Corpus averages hide change (a feature can keep its mean while every document moves), so we
compare each document with its own T0 on ten feature families, computed from one spaCy parse.
\emph{Lexical (skin):} word choice (Jaccard), 198-word function-word profile, 12-mark punctuation
profile, lexical richness (MATTR, word length).
\emph{Syntactic (skeleton):} sentence shape (mean, s.d.\ of length), POS distribution,
dependency-label distribution, tree depth (mean/max depth, dependency distance), Flesch--Kincaid
grade.
\emph{Meaning (hull):} SBERT. Distributions are compared with Jensen--Shannon distance, scalars
with the RMS difference of $z$-scores.

\textbf{One scale.} Each distance is divided by the distance between the T0 and five unrelated T0s
of the same domain, giving retention
\[
R = 1 - d(\mathrm{T}k,\mathrm{T0})\,/\,d(\mathrm{unrelated},\mathrm{T0}).
\]
$R=1$ means unchanged, $R=0$ as different as another author's document, and $R<0$ pushed beyond
it. We report 95\% bootstrap CIs and paired one-sided Wilcoxon tests (style distance $>$ meaning
distance) with Holm correction.

\textbf{Style goes, meaning stays} (Figures~\ref{fig:delta} and~\ref{fig:retention}). For ChatGPT every style family
keeps $\le$0.34 at T3 (most $\approx$0.2) while meaning keeps 0.85 (95\% CI 0.85--0.86). Meaning is
the most retained family for every LLM paraphraser, and in 61 of 63 paraphraser $\times$ family
tests at T1 the style distance is significantly larger than the meaning distance (Holm
$p<0.05$); both exceptions are WordNet (function words, sentence shape), whose word swaps leave
those features in place.
\textbf{Gone in the first pass.} For ChatGPT and PaLM2, 82--92\% of each style family's
three-round loss happens at T1.
\textbf{The skeleton goes with the skin.} Syntax is not more durable than vocabulary: mean
lexical vs.\ syntactic retention is 0.15 vs.\ 0.14 for ChatGPT and 0.33 vs.\ 0.30 for PaLM2.
Pegasus-full restructures most (syntactic $-0.53$; tree depth $-2.27$).

\textbf{Overwritten, not just erased.} Negative retention marks a consistent shift in one
direction (Table~\ref{tab:shifts}). ChatGPT formalises (reading grade 9.8$\to$12.4, $d_z=1.05$;
word length $d_z=1.50$), DIPPER does the reverse (shorter words, more determiners), and Pegasus
splits sentences and flattens parse trees ($d_z=-1.36$). Because the direction identifies the
paraphraser, this is early evidence for RQ3: authorial identity is not merely erased but replaced.

\textbf{Preliminary answer to RQ1.} Under LLM paraphrasing, style is replaced long before meaning:
after one pass of ChatGPT, PaLM2 or DIPPER the style families have moved 55--90\% of the way to
an unrelated author's style while 79--88\% of the meaning is retained, and the syntactic skeleton
is replaced as fast as the lexical skin.
