\section{Dataset}

\subsection{The Ship of Theseus Corpus}

We use the paraphrased corpus released by Tripto et al.~\cite{tripto2024ship}, organised along
three axes:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep,topsep=2pt,leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Seven datasets (domains):} XSUM (BBC news), Yelp (reviews), CMV (Reddit
        ChangeMyView opinions), ELI5 (Reddit explanatory answers), SciGen (scientific text),
        TLDR (Reddit summaries) and WP (Reddit WritingPrompts stories).
  \item \textbf{Seven sources} that wrote the original T0: Human and six LLMs (OpenAI, LLaMA,
        PaLM, Tsinghua, BigScience, Eleuther-AI) prompted with the first 30 tokens of the human
        text, so human and LLM texts from one prompt share a document \texttt{key}.
  \item \textbf{Seven paraphraser settings} of four models, each applied three times
        (T0$\to$T1$\to$T2$\to$T3): ChatGPT (GPT-3.5-turbo), PaLM2, Pegasus~\cite{zhang2020pegasus}
        (\emph{full}: every sentence; \emph{slight}: 25\% of sentences) and
        DIPPER~\cite{krishna2023paraphrasing} (default, \emph{low} and \emph{high} diversity).
\end{itemize}
Each dataset has two files (14 in total). The \textbf{paraphrased} file
($\approx$420k rows) holds every original with its 21 paraphrases, with columns
\texttt{source}, \texttt{key}, \texttt{text} and \texttt{version\_name}
(\texttt{original}, \texttt{chatgpt}, \texttt{chatgpt\_chatgpt}, \ldots). The \textbf{train} file
($\approx$19.5k rows) holds un-paraphrased texts from the same sources; we verified that it shares
no key with the paraphrased file. The paraphrased files serve RQ1, RQ3 and the RQ2 test set; the
train files train the RQ2 attribution models. As a controlled reference we add a rule-based
\textbf{WordNet} baseline that replaces 25/38/50\% of content words with same-POS synonyms at
T1/T2/T3.

\subsection{Preprocessing}

All 14 files pass through one logged pipeline (\url{src/data/preprocess.py}):
\textbf{(1) Cleaning}: HTML entities decoded, Unicode NFKC-normalised, curly quotes and dashes
mapped to ASCII, whitespace collapsed. Sources format text differently (DIPPER outputs begin with
a space; some models emit curly quotes), so without this step punctuation features would measure
formatting rather than style; $\approx$44\% of rows changed. We deliberately do \emph{not}
lower-case, remove stop-words, stem or truncate, since that would destroy the stylistic signal
under study.
\textbf{(2) Chain alignment}: one row per (source, key) with T0 and all 21 paraphrases, keeping
only documents complete for all seven settings so every paraphraser is compared on the same texts
(retention 90--97\%; SciGen 58\% and WP 69\% because many PaLM chains are missing).
\textbf{(3) Length filter}: T0 under 30 words removed ($\approx$2\%).
\textbf{(4) Sanity checks}: T1 outputs sharing $<$10\% of their words with T0 are flagged. This
exposed a corpus artifact: in five of seven domains 54--95\% of \textbf{DIPPER (high)} outputs are
unrelated to their source (e.g.\ narrative fiction in place of a BBC article), so we exclude that
setting from interpretation.
\textbf{(5) Leakage-free splits}: train files split 85/15 by key, so a human text and the LLM texts
from its prompt never straddle the split.

The result is 16{,}386 aligned chains (2{,}269 Human) and 19{,}200 training texts. Because RQ1
concerns the decay of a human author's identity, the analyses below use a fixed sample of
\textbf{100 Human documents per domain} (700 in total, seed 42; Table~\ref{tab:corpus_stats}).
