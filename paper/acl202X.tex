\begin{abstract}
A machine paraphrase keeps \emph{what} a text says while rewriting \emph{how} it says it. We ask
a Ship-of-Theseus question for authorship: after three rounds of LLM paraphrasing, does a document
still carry its author's identity? On 700 human-written documents from seven domains of the Ship of
Theseus corpus, paraphrased by seven LLM settings and a rule-based baseline, we find that wording
is replaced in a single pass while meaning is largely kept. Comparing ten lexical, syntactic and
semantic feature families on a common scale (retention relative to an unrelated document), style is
replaced long before meaning: the syntactic skeleton goes as fast as the lexical skin, and each
paraphraser overwrites the author's style with its own.
\end{abstract}
